## First comment (post right after submitting)

```
Hi HN, I made PianoCheetah.

When Guitar Hero came out I was working on a plain midi app. The
guitar part wasn't real, but it was fun. The drums were basically
real. The keyboard part was... not great. So I changed direction and
built this instead.

The idea: take a real song's piano part and simplify it so your
hands never move. Each hand gets at most one note at a time, mapped
onto the 5 fingers it's already resting on, so 10 keys total. The
notes are drawn lined up with your fingers in a pianoroll view
instead of standard notation.

The part I like best is that you set the tempo and the feel
(velocities), and the rest of the tracks follow you. Slow down and
the band slows down. So it feels like playing the song, not playing
along to a recording you can't keep up with.

Some details:

- Works with any midi keyboard or drum pad
- Any track in any midi file can become a practice song
- Built-in software synth if you don't have a sound module
- Not a DAW, on purpose. You're practicing, not mixing.
- Linux only right now, installs from Flathub (search pianocheetah in
  KDE Discover or GNOME Software)
- Free, MIT licensed: https://github.com/Stephen-Hazel/PianoCheetah

It used to be called PianoCheater. Intro video:
https://youtu.be/1Qh76D03y4Q?t=155

I'd love feedback, especially from people who tried learning piano
and quit, and from anyone who knows midi well enough to tell me
where the simplification gets music wrong.
```
