# 60 sec demo video plan

Goal: show one thing - someone who can't play piano is playing a real
song, and the band follows them.

## Shot list

| Time   | What's on screen |
|--------|------------------|
| 0-5s   | Clair de Lune's full piano roll (screen.png view), a wall of notes.  Caption: "This is Clair de Lune. Nope." |
| 5-10s  | Changes to the simplified view (screen_ez.png).  Caption: "This is Clair de Lune in PianoCheetah. 10 keys. Hands don't move." |
| 10-25s | Split screen: hands on a cheap midi keyboard on one side, the screen on the other.  Melody played slowly, a bit clumsily.  Caption: "You set the tempo..." |
| 25-40s | Key moment: player slows down or hesitates, backing tracks slow down with them, then speed up again.  Caption: "...the band follows you."  Audio must be clear here. |
| 40-50s | Quick cut to a second, more recognizable song, played the same way.  Optionally a few seconds of drum pad. |
| 50-60s | End card: "PianoCheetah - free, open source, on Flathub" + pianocheetah.app |

## Tips

- Film real hands.  Screen recording alone looks like any app.  Hands
  plus screen makes it believable.
- Don't play perfectly.  A beginner is more convincing.  If possible,
  film a friend or family member who doesn't play.
- No voiceover.  Short captions work on silent autoplay (Reddit,
  Twitter, Mastodon), and you keep the music audio.
- Make the tempo-follow moment obvious - other piano apps don't have
  it, so it needs to read even with sound off.  Maybe put a tempo
  number on screen that visibly drops.
- Song choice: Clair de Lune is public domain, so safe.  A modern pop
  hit gets more attention but has copyright risk on YouTube.  Use
  public-domain / traditional songs for the main video, maybe pop in
  short clips.
- Make a vertical 9:16 version for Shorts, TikTok, Reels.  Keyboard in
  the bottom half, screen in the top half.
